## Algebraic Field Extensions

Def:Let $L|F$ be a field extension,which means $(L,+,\cdot)$ is a field and $(F,+,\cdot)$ is a subfield

(a):An element $a\in L$ is called algebraic over $F$ if $\exists f(x)\in F[x]$ such that $f(a)=0$.

(b):$L|F$ is called algebraic field extension if $\forall x\in L$,$x$ is algebraic over $F$.

example:$\mathbb{Q}[i]=\{a+ib|a,b\in \mathbb{Q}\}$,and $\mathbb{Q}(x):=\frac{p}{q},p,q\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$.$\mathbb{Q}[i]|\mathbb{Q}$ is an algebraic extension while $\mathbb{Q}(x)$ is not because $x$ is not algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$.
