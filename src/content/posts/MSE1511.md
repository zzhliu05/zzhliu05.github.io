## Algebraic Field Extensions

Def:Let $L|F$ be a field extension,which means $(L,+,\cdot)$ is a field and $(F,+,\cdot)$ is a subfield

(a):An element $a\in L$ is called algebraic over $F$ if $\exists f(x)\in F[x]$ such that $f(a)=0$.

(b):$L|F$ is called algebraic field extension if $\forall x\in L$,$x$ is algebraic over $F$.

example:$\mathbb{Q}[i]=\{a+ib|a,b\in \mathbb{Q}\}$,and $\mathbb{Q}(x):=\frac{p}{q},p,q\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$.$\mathbb{Q}[i]|\mathbb{Q}$ is an algebraic extension while $\mathbb{Q}(x)$ is not because $x$ is not algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Galois Extensions

Def:An alg ext $L|F$ is called normal if every embedding $\phi:L\to \bar{L}$ with $\phi|_F=id_F$ satiisfies $\phi(L)=L$.

Example:

(a):$E=\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})|\mathbb{Q}$ is not normal.One can let
$$
\phi(2^{1/3})=2^{1/3}\omega
$$
We can check $\phi|_\mathbb{Q}=id_\mathbb{Q}$,but $im(\phi)\neq E$.

(b):$L=\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3},\omega)|\mathbb{Q}$ is normal,because any field embedding $\phi:L\to \mathbb{C}$ (which automatically satisfies $\phi|_{\mathbb{Q}})=id$,then
$$
\phi(2^{1/3})=2^{1/3}\omega\in L,\phi(\omega)=\omega^2\in L
$$


remark:It suffices to show $\forall \phi,\phi(L)\subset L$.

proof:Suppose the remark is true.$\forall \phi$,we can extend $\phi$ onto $\bar{L}$.
$$
\bar{\phi}:\bar{L}\to \bar{L}
$$
Notice that $\bar{\phi}(\bar{L})\subset \bar{L}$ is algebraic close,but $\bar{L}$ is already the "smallest" algebraic closed field extension.So $\bar{\phi}$ is surjective.

Notation:Let $L|F$ be a field extension.$Aut(L|F)$ is all the field automorphisms that is identity on $F$.
$$
Aut(L|F)=\{\phi:L\to L|\phi|_{F}=id|_{F}\}
$$

excercise:$L=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$,then $\exists ! \mathbb{F}_p\subset E\subset L,[E:\mathbb{F}_p]=p^n$.

Def:$L|F$ an algebraic field extension is called Galois if it is separable and normal.

$$
G(L|F)=Aut(L|F)
$$
is the Galois group of $L|F$.

Example:

(a):$Aut(\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})|\mathbb{Q})=1$

(b):$Aut(L:=\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3},\omega)|\mathbb{Q})$ is separable because $\mathbb{Q}$ is perfect.And it is normal.
$$
|G(L|\mathbb{Q})|=[L:\mathbb{Q}]=6
$$
$[L:\mathbb{Q}]=6$ because $[\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}):\mathbb{Q}]=3$,and $[L:\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})]=2$,because $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$.So by tower law
$$
[L:\mathbb{Q}]=6
$$
We will show
$$
G(L|\mathbb{Q})=S_3
$$
proof:It suffices to show that the group is non-abelian.
$$
\phi:\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3})\to L,\phi(2^{1/3})=\omega 2^{1/3}
$$

Further observations:We split the intermediate field
$$
(L|I|\mathbb{Q}),I=\mathbb{Q},L,\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}),\mathbb{Q}(2^{1/3}\omega),\mathbb{Q}(\omega^2 2^{1/3}),\mathbb{Q}(\omega)
$$
There are $6$ possible type of intermediate fields.We claim that they correspond to subgroups of $S_3$ (there are exactly $6$ subgroups of $S_3$).

There are exactly $3$ normal subgroups of $S_3$,and we have exactly $3$ normal extensions over $\mathbb{Q}$.
